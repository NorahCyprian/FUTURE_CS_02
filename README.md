FUTURE_CS_02 - Phishing Email Detection & Awareness Report 
 

Project Overview 
Phishing is one of the most common and dangerous cyber threats used by attackers to steal sensitive information such as usernames, passwords, banking details, and payment information. Cybercriminals often disguise themselves as trusted organizations like banks, social media platforms, or payment services to trick users into clicking malicious links or providing confidential information. 
In this task, I acted as a Cyber Security Analyst by analyzing multiple phishing email samples, identifying common phishing indicators, classifying the threat level, and creating an awareness report that organizations can use to educate employees about phishing attacks. 







Phishing Email Samples Analyzed 
1. Social Media Account Verification Scam 
This phishing email pretended to come from a social media platform and claimed that the user's account required verification to avoid suspension. The attacker attempted to create panic by warning the user that failure to verify the account within 24 hours would result in permanent account deactivation. 
•	Fake sender example: security@social-verification-alert.com 
•	Suspicious link example: http://social-account-check.xyz 
•	Main objective: Steal login credentials 

Red Flags Identified 
•	Fake sender domain 
•	Urgent verification request 
•	Threat of account suspension 
•	Suspicious external link 
•	Generic greeting such as ‘Dear User’ 

2. Payment Notification Scam 
This phishing email claimed that a payment had been successfully processed or that an invoice was pending. The message encouraged the victim to click a link to review transaction details or cancel unauthorized activity. 
•	Fake sender example: billing@secure-paymentnotice.com 
•	Suspicious link example: http://payment-review-secure.xyz 
•	Main objective: Steal financial information or credentials 
Red Flags Identified 
•	Fake billing domain 
•	Suspicious payment notification 
•	Fear tactics involving unauthorized transactions 
•	Links directing users to fake payment portals 
•	Generic greetings and poor formatting 


3. Bank Security Alert Scam 
This phishing email impersonated a bank and claimed unusual activity had been detected on the user's account. The victim was instructed to verify their identity immediately to avoid account suspension. 
•	Fake sender example: alerts@bank-securityteam.com 
•	Suspicious link example: http://secure-bank-login.xyz 
•	Main objective: Capture online banking credentials 
Red Flags Identified 
•	Fake banking domain 
•	Urgent security warning 
•	Request for sensitive information 
•	Suspicious login page 
•	Fear-based language to pressure victims 




Key Findings 
After analyzing all phishing samples, the following common phishing indicators were identified: 
1. Fake Sender Domains 
All emails used fake domains designed to resemble legitimate organizations. Attackers commonly register lookalike domains to deceive users into believing the emails are genuine. 
Examples 
•	@social-verification-alert.com 
•	@secure-paymentnotice.com 
•	@bank-securityteam.com 
Legitimate companies usually use official domains that exactly match their organization name. 

2. Suspicious Links 
All emails contained suspicious links leading to unknown or misleading domains. Many phishing campaigns use cheap or unusual domain extensions such as .xyz, .top, or .online. 
Examples 
•	social-account-check.xyz 
•	payment-review-secure.xyz 
•	secure-bank-login.xyz 
Users should always hover over links before clicking to inspect the real destination. 

3. Urgency and Fear Tactics 
The phishing emails attempted to pressure victims into acting quickly without thinking carefully. 
Common Examples 
•	“Your account will be suspended.” 
•	“Unauthorized payment detected.” 
•	“Verify your account immediately.” 
•	“Failure to respond within 24 hours may result in account closure.” 
Attackers use fear and urgency to manipulate victims into clicking malicious links. 


4. Generic Greetings 
The emails used generic greetings instead of the recipient’s real name. 
Examples 
•	“Dear User” 
•	“Dear Customer” 
•	“Dear Valued Client” 
Legitimate organizations usually personalize emails with the customer’s actual name. 


5. Grammar and Spelling Errors 
Some emails contained spelling mistakes, awkward wording, or poor grammar, which are common indicators of phishing attempts. 
Example 
•	“Your account has been temporary suspended.” 
•	“Confirm you're account now.” 
Professional companies rarely send emails with obvious grammatical errors.

Phishing Prevention Tips 
Always Verify Sender Addresses 
Carefully inspect the sender’s email address before trusting any message. Legitimate companies use official domains. 
Avoid Clicking Suspicious Links 
Hover over links before clicking to verify the destination URL. Do not open unknown or shortened links. 
Never Share Passwords Through Email 
Legitimate companies will never ask users to provide passwords, banking details, or verification codes through email. 
Watch for Urgent Messages 
Be cautious of emails demanding immediate action, threatening suspension, or creating panic. 
Report Suspicious Emails 
If you suspect phishing: 
1.	Do not click links 
2.	Do not download attachments 
3.	Report the email to the IT or security department 
4.	Delete the message immediately 



Conclusion 
This task demonstrated how phishing attacks use social engineering techniques such as fake sender domains, urgent messages, suspicious links, and fear tactics to deceive users. All analyzed samples were identified as phishing emails with high risk. 
The awareness report developed from this analysis can help organizations educate employees, improve cybersecurity awareness, and reduce the risk of phishing attacks.
